# How dental costs work in Singapore

Dental treatment in Singapore is delivered across four broad care settings, each with different pricing, subsidy access, and patient mix. Understanding which setting you are looking at is the first step to reading any dental cost figure correctly.

## The four care settings

- **Private dental clinics** — the most common setting. About 80% of dental care in Singapore is delivered here. Fees are set by each clinic and vary; the Ministry of Health publishes **fee benchmarks** as a reference for what routine and typical cases cost in primary care private clinics.
- **CHAS clinics** — private dental clinics that participate in the Community Health Assist Scheme. Patients with CHAS Blue, CHAS Orange, Pioneer Generation, or Merdeka Generation cards receive subsidies on 18 specified procedures at these clinics.
- **Polyclinics** — government-run primary care clinics that provide subsidised dental services to Singapore Citizens (with deeper discounts for children and elderly), Permanent Residents, and non-residents.
- **Restructured (public) hospitals and the National Dental Centre Singapore (NDCS)** — for more complex or specialist care. Patients can be treated as private (non-subsidised) or subsidised; subsidised access typically requires a referral letter dated before the consultation.

## How the MOH fee benchmarks work

In August 2025 the Dental Fee Benchmarking Advisory Committee published recommended fee benchmarks for **18 common dental procedures** (the same 18 procedures that are subsidised under CHAS). The Ministry of Health accepted the recommendations on 1 September 2025, and from 1 October 2025 CHAS dental clinics are required to display the benchmarks alongside their own price lists.

The benchmarks are **a reference, not a strict cap**. They cover routine and typical cases performed in private dental clinics in the primary care setting, exclude GST, and account for common variations in patient condition and complexity. Actual fees can vary based on clinical factors, materials, or case complexity.

## What a fee usually includes

A quoted procedure fee at a private clinic typically covers the procedure itself and the materials directly used. It does **not** usually include:

- The consultation that diagnosed the problem
- X-rays needed to plan or perform the procedure
- Laboratory work (for crowns, dentures, bridges)
- Follow-up review visits
- Re-treatment if the original treatment fails

When asking a clinic for a quote, it's worth confirming what is and isn't included before agreeing.

At public healthcare institutions, the published average fees similarly **exclude consultation, X-rays, investigation, and re-treatment fees** unless otherwise noted.

## GST handling

GST in Singapore is currently 9% (raised from 8% on 1 January 2024). GST treatment varies by setting:

- **Private clinics** — fees usually quoted exclusive of GST; check whether GST is added at billing
- **Polyclinics** — fees displayed inclusive of GST for non-subsidised patients; GST is **absorbed by the government** for subsidised Singapore Citizen and PR patients
- **Restructured hospitals (including NDCS)** — same as polyclinics: private rates include GST, subsidised rates have GST absorbed

If you have a CHAS card or Medisave eligibility, the figures you actually pay out-of-pocket can be substantially below the headline fee.

## Three figures to look at when planning treatment

For any procedure your dentist or screening report flags, three figures together give you the best picture of what to expect:

1. **The MOH benchmark range** for the procedure in private primary care
2. **The relevant subsidy cap** from CHAS, Medisave, or another scheme you are eligible for
3. **The public hospital average** (for more complex or specialist procedures, or if you anticipate referral)

The chatbot has separate entries with each of these — see the procedure-specific cost entries (`cost-cleaning`, `cost-crown`, `cost-implant`, etc.) and the scheme-specific subsidy entries (`subsidy-chas-blue`, `subsidy-medisave-dental`, etc.).

## What this entry does *not* cover

Specific clinic-by-clinic fees, exact subsidy amounts you will personally receive, and guaranteed quotes are *not* in scope of any chatbot response. The MOH benchmarks and published institutional fees are reference points; the only person who can give you an actual binding quote is the clinic processing your treatment.

# Where to get dental treatment in Singapore

Singapore has four broad dental care settings. Each suits different needs — what's affordable for a routine cleaning is not the same as what's appropriate for an implant or major surgical procedure. The right setting depends on the complexity of the treatment, your subsidy eligibility, and whether referral is required.

## Private dental clinics

The most common setting. About 80% of dental care in Singapore is delivered in private clinics — both standalone practices and large chain clinics.

- **Suits:** routine and typical cases, walk-in access, choice of dentist, evening or weekend appointments.
- **Cost reference:** the **MOH 2025 fee benchmarks** for 18 common procedures cover this setting. Fees can vary by clinic, materials, and case complexity.
- **Subsidies:** none unless the clinic is also a CHAS clinic.
- **Referral:** not required.

## CHAS clinics

Private dental clinics that have signed up to the Community Health Assist Scheme. Operated by the same dentists; the only difference is the subsidy infrastructure.

- **Suits:** Singapore Citizens with CHAS Blue, CHAS Orange, Pioneer Generation, or Merdeka Generation cards.
- **Cost reference:** the same MOH benchmarks apply; subsidy is deducted from the bill.
- **Subsidies:** 18 procedures are subsidised, with caps that vary by card type. From 1 October 2025, CHAS clinics are required to display the MOH benchmarks alongside their price lists.
- **Referral:** not required.
- **Locator:** [chas.sg](https://www.chas.sg) lists participating clinics.

## Polyclinics

Government-run primary care clinics with dental services. Operated by three healthcare clusters: NHG Polyclinics (north), SingHealth Polyclinics (central / east / south), and NUHS Polyclinics (west).

- **Suits:** routine dental care including examinations, cleanings, fillings, extractions, and X-rays. Polyclinics generally do not handle complex specialist work — that gets referred to a restructured hospital or NDCS.
- **Cost reference:** subsidised rates for Singapore Citizens, deeper subsidies for children under 18 and elderly aged 65+. Permanent Residents pay an intermediate rate; non-residents pay full private rates.
- **Subsidies:** built-in subsidy for Singapore Citizens; CHAS and Medisave are not generally used at polyclinics for routine dental procedures.
- **Referral:** not required for routine work.

## Restructured hospitals and the National Dental Centre Singapore (NDCS)

For more complex, surgical, or specialist dental care. The institutions that publish dental fees include Alexandra Hospital, Changi General Hospital, Khoo Teck Puat Hospital, KK Women's and Children's Hospital, the National Dental Centre Singapore, the National University Centre for Oral Health Singapore (NUCOH), Ng Teng Fong General Hospital, Sengkang General Hospital, and Tan Tock Seng Hospital.

- **Suits:** specialist procedures (root canal on molars, complex crowns, implants, full dentures, impacted wisdom tooth surgery, orthodontics, periodontal surgery, oral and maxillofacial surgery).
- **Cost reference:** the MOH publishes 2024 average fees for non-subsidised (private) patients across these institutions for selected complex procedures — see [MOH Guide to Dental Treatment Costs](https://www.moh.gov.sg/managing-expenses/bills-and-fee-benchmarks/guide-to-dental-treatment-costs/).
- **Subsidies:** subsidised rates available for Singapore Citizens with a referral letter dated before the consultation. Subsidy levels depend on means-testing.
- **Medisave:** can be used for surgical procedures (implant insertion, wisdom tooth surgery, etc.) within scheme limits.
- **Referral:** required for subsidised access. Walk-in is possible at private rates.

## Quick comparison

| Setting 			| Best for 					| Subsidy 					| Referral required |
|---				|----------------------------			|---						|-------------------|
| Private clinic		| Routine work, convenience, choice of dentist	| None unless CHAS 				| No 		    |

## How the same procedure compares across settings

Some practical patterns to keep in mind:

- **Routine cleaning** is almost always cheaper at a polyclinic than at a private clinic; CHAS clinics with subsidy can also be very affordable.
- **A simple filling** is similarly cheaper at a polyclinic. The MOH benchmark for a private filling is $65–$160 (simple) or $80–$180 (complex), excluding GST.
- **A crown** typically costs more at a restructured hospital than at a private clinic on a like-for-like basis. The MOH benchmark for a private permanent crown is $750–$1,400. Restructured hospital averages for a single crown range from approximately $804 to $1,491 across institutions, before subsidy.
- **Implants** are not typically cheap anywhere. Restructured hospital averages range roughly $4,500 to $6,600 per unit (implant + crown). Medisave can be used for the surgical portion.

## Choosing a setting for your situation

A few practical guides:

- **If your screening flagged Initial-stage findings** (early plaque, mild concerns), a polyclinic or CHAS clinic is usually appropriate.
- **If your screening flagged Moderate to Advanced findings**, a private clinic, CHAS clinic, or polyclinic is appropriate as a first visit. The dentist there can refer you on if specialist work is needed.
- **If your screening flagged Advanced Cavity, multiple missing teeth, or other findings likely to need specialist attention**, expect to be referred to a restructured hospital or NDCS — but the first visit can still be at a primary-care setting unless symptoms are urgent.
- **If you are eligible for CHAS, PG, or MG subsidies**, a CHAS clinic is typically the most cost-effective setting for the 18 covered procedures.

# Cost — professional cleaning (scaling and polishing)

Professional cleaning typically combines two procedures: *scaling* (removal of plaque and calculus from the tooth surfaces, particularly at and below the gum line) and *polishing* (smoothing the tooth surfaces and removing surface stains). They are usually billed as separate line items.

For background on what the procedure involves, see [referral-treatment-cleaning](referral-treatment-cleaning).

## Private dental clinics — MOH 2025 fee benchmark

The MOH Dental Fee Benchmarking Advisory Committee's published reference range for routine and typical cases in private primary care clinics (excluding GST):

| Procedure 	| Lower bound 	| Upper bound 	|
|---		|---		|---		|
| Scaling 	| S$35 		| S$60 		|
| Polishing 	| S$26 		| S$38 		|

A combined scaling and polishing visit at a private clinic therefore typically falls in the S$61 to S$98 range before GST, before any consultation fee, and before any X-rays if needed.

The benchmark is a reference, not a strict cap. Fees may be higher for cases with heavier calculus build-up, gum disease, or other complicating factors. From 1 October 2025, CHAS dental clinics are required to display these benchmarks alongside their own price lists.

## Polyclinics — SingHealth Polyclinics charges

| CHAS Orange 		| S$13.50 	| S$20.00 |

For full scheme details see [subsidy-chas-blue](subsidy-chas-blue), [subsidy-chas-orange](subsidy-chas-orange), [subsidy-pioneer-generation](subsidy-pioneer-generation), or [subsidy-merdeka-generation](subsidy-merdeka-generation).

## What's typically not included

A scaling and polishing visit usually does not automatically include:

- The consultation fee for the visit (often a separate line item)
- X-rays if the dentist takes any during the visit
- Topical fluoride application (often offered, sometimes priced separately)

When booking a cleaning, ask whether the quoted price is for the procedure only or for a complete cleaning visit.

## What if more than scaling and polishing is needed?

If your gums show signs of moderate or advanced gum disease, the dentist may recommend a deeper procedure — *scaling and root planing* (sometimes called a "deep cleaning") — that goes below the gum line into periodontal pockets. This is a separate procedure not currently covered under the standard CHAS scaling subsidy and is typically performed across two or more visits.

For users whose screening report flagged Moderate or Advanced plaque, the in-person dental examination determines whether a standard cleaning is sufficient or whether scaling and root planing is appropriate.

# Cost — dental consultation

Consultation is the first appointment where the dentist examines your teeth and gums, takes any X-rays needed, and proposes a treatment plan. It is almost always billed separately from the procedure itself — even if you have a procedure done in the same visit, the consultation typically has its own fee.

# Cost — permanent crown

A permanent crown is the most common single-procedure cost-driver on a dental treatment plan. Crowns vary by material — porcelain, porcelain-fused-to-metal, full metal, zirconia — but the MOH benchmark covers a single representative range across the typical material mix for routine cases.

For background on what a crown is and when it's recommended, see [referral-treatment-crown](referral-treatment-crown).

## Flexi-MediSave (from mid-2026)

From mid-2026, seniors aged 60 and above can tap on **Flexi-MediSave** to offset up to **S$400 per year** for root canal treatments and permanent crowns at CHAS dental clinics and public healthcare institutions. This is in addition to any CHAS subsidy. See [subsidy-flexi-medisave](subsidy-flexi-medisave).

For users below 60 years old, Medisave does not generally cover permanent crowns — they remain non-surgical restorative procedures outside the standard Medisave scope.

## What's typically not included

A crown fee usually does **not** include:

- The build-up filling or post-and-core if needed before the crown
- The root canal that may have preceded the crown — see [cost-root-canal](cost-root-canal)
- The temporary crown worn between visits (sometimes included, sometimes separate)
- Adjustments at follow-up visits beyond the standard fitting

For a full picture, ask the clinic for an itemised quote covering preparation, lab fees, the crown itself, and follow-up.

## Combined treatment plans

A common treatment combination is **root canal + crown** on the same tooth (typical when an Advanced Cavity is too deep for a filling alone). Total private benchmark range for a molar: approximately S$1,622–S$2,800 (S$872–S$1,400 root canal + S$750–S$1,400 crown), excluding GST and excluding consultation and X-ray fees.

For users with CHAS subsidy plus Flexi-MediSave (from mid-2026 for seniors aged 60+), the out-of-pocket cost can be substantially below this.

# Cost — simple tooth extraction

This entry covers **simple extractions** — removal of a tooth that is fully erupted and can be removed with forceps and gentle pressure under local anaesthetic. For surgical extractions (impacted wisdom teeth, broken roots, or teeth requiring incision and bone removal), see [cost-wisdom-tooth-surgery](cost-wisdom-tooth-surgery), which covers the surgical extraction category that is Medisave-claimable.

## Medisave

Medisave generally **does not** cover simple extractions — they are considered routine non-surgical procedures. Medisave applies to **surgical** extractions (wisdom teeth, broken roots, retained roots requiring surgical removal). See [subsidy-medisave-dental](subsidy-medisave-dental) and [cost-wisdom-tooth-surgery](cost-wisdom-tooth-surgery).

## When a simple extraction becomes surgical

Some extractions that look simple at the start can become surgical if the tooth fractures during removal or if access turns out to be more difficult than expected. In those cases the procedure (and the fee) escalate. The dentist will usually pause and discuss this before proceeding.

If your tooth is broken below the gum line, has had a previous root canal, or is severely curved on X-ray, expect a surgical extraction from the outset rather than a simple one.

# How to tell if a dental treatment is Medisave-claimable — the TOSP system

The **Table of Surgical Procedures (TOSP)** is a Ministry of Health master list of surgical procedures eligible for Medisave and MediShield Life claims. Each procedure on the list has a **TOSP code** (a short alphanumeric identifier) and a **TOSP Table classification** (1A through 7C, plus MSP) that reflects the procedure's complexity. The Table classification determines the maximum amount that can be claimed from Medisave.

For dental, this list is the practical answer to "Is my treatment Medisave-claimable?" If a procedure has a TOSP code, it is in principle Medisave-claimable subject to scheme rules and the patient's eligibility. If it does not, it is not.

For the dollar amounts of common dental TOSP claims, see the companion entry [subsidy-medisave-dental](subsidy-medisave-dental).

## Quick read

- **156 dental-relevant TOSP codes** exist in the master list — covering tooth surgery, gum surgery, implants, jaw and bone surgery, TMJ procedures, salivary gland surgery, tongue and lip surgery, cleft repairs, and oral cancer surgery.
- **Routine dental work (fillings, scaling, polishing, simple non-surgical extractions) does not have TOSP codes** — these are not Medisave-claimable. Look to CHAS for these (see [subsidy-chas-blue](subsidy-chas-blue), [subsidy-chas-orange](subsidy-chas-orange), [subsidy-pioneer-generation](subsidy-pioneer-generation), [subsidy-merdeka-generation](subsidy-merdeka-generation)).
- **Surgical procedures that are typically Medisave-claimable** include impacted wisdom tooth removal, dental implant insertion, bone grafting, sinus lift, periodontal flap surgery, gum grafting, and most jaw surgery.

## TOSP code format

Dental-relevant TOSP codes use the prefixes:

- **SF8xxT / SF7xxT** — Tooth procedures (Digestive system)
- **SF8xxM / SF7xxM** — Mouth procedures (Digestive system) — periodontium, abscess, cyst, etc.
- **SF8xxP / SF7xxP** — Parotid (salivary) gland procedures
- **SF8xxT (different range) / SF7xxT** — Tongue procedures
- **SB8xxM / SB7xxM** — Mandible / Maxilla / jaw bone procedures (Musculoskeletal system)
- **SB8xxJ / SB7xxJ** — Joints (TMJ) procedures (Musculoskeletal system)

The "M", "T", "J", "P" suffixes indicate the anatomical part. The numerical portion identifies the specific procedure.

## TOSP Table classification

Each TOSP code carries a Table classification (1A through 7C, plus MSP — "Minor Surgical Procedure"). The Table reflects:

- Procedure duration
- Complexity
- Surgical skill required
- Resources used (operating theatre, equipment)

Higher Tables = more complex procedures = higher Medisave claim limits. The exact dollar limits per Table for dental are not separately published by the CPF Board — practical reference figures are in [subsidy-medisave-dental](subsidy-medisave-dental).

- Routine root canal treatment (except via Flexi-MediSave for seniors aged 60+)
- Routine denture fabrication
- Veneers, teeth whitening, cosmetic procedures
- Orthodontic treatment (braces)

For these, look to CHAS, polyclinic-subsidised pricing, or out-of-pocket payment.

# Medisave for dental

Medisave is the national medical savings scheme administered by the CPF Board. For dental, **Medisave covers surgical procedures only** — routine non-surgical work (fillings, scaling, polishing, simple extractions) is not Medisave-claimable. This is a frequent source of confusion.

Two important things to understand together:

- **What's claimable** is determined by the **Table of Surgical Procedures (TOSP)** — a Ministry of Health master list of procedures eligible for Medisave / MediShield Life claims. If a dental procedure has a TOSP code, it is in principle Medisave-claimable; if it does not, it is not. See [subsidy-medisave-dental-tosp](subsidy-medisave-dental-tosp) for the dental codes by category.
- **The dollar amount** that can actually be claimed is determined by the procedure's TOSP Table classification (1A through 7C, plus MSP) and CPF Board scheme rules. The CPF Board does not publish a per-procedure dental claim-limit table the way it does for some medical procedures — the dollar figures circulating publicly come from clinic-published references rather than a single canonical document.

The figures in this entry are practical reference amounts compiled from a Singapore dental clinic's published reference. Treat them as approximate maxima — the clinic processing your treatment can confirm the actual claimable amount for your case.

## Eligibility

To use Medisave for dental:

- You must be a **Singapore Citizen** or **Permanent Resident**
- You must be at least **18 years old**
- You must have sufficient Medisave balance (in your own account or, where allowed, an immediate family member's account — spouse, parents, children, or in some cases siblings)

4. You can use your own Medisave or a family member's, depending on the relationship and the procedure category.

# CHAS Blue — dental subsidies

The Community Health Assist Scheme (CHAS) is a Singapore Government scheme that provides healthcare subsidies to eligible Singapore Citizens at participating clinics. Cards are issued in tiers based on means-testing. **CHAS Blue** is the standard tier for lower-middle-income households.

CHAS dental subsidies were enhanced from **1 October 2025**. The figures in this entry reflect the enhanced caps; older guides still circulating online may show pre-2025 figures.

## Eligibility

CHAS Blue is generally available to Singapore Citizens with monthly household income per person of approximately S$1,200 to S$2,000 (or, for households with no income, an Annual Value of home approximately S$13,001 to S$21,000). Final eligibility is determined by the means-test administered by the Agency for Integrated Care (AIC).

Eligibility can be checked or applied for at [chas.sg](https://www.chas.sg) or via Healthhub.

## Where the subsidy applies

CHAS dental subsidies apply at **CHAS dental clinics** — private clinics that have signed up to participate in the scheme. The subsidy is deducted from the bill at the point of treatment. CHAS subsidies generally do not apply at polyclinics or restructured hospitals (which have their own subsidy structures).

A clinic locator showing CHAS-participating dental clinics is available at [chas.sg](https://www.chas.sg).

## Covered procedures and subsidy caps (effective 1 October 2025)

The 18 dental procedures covered, with maximum subsidy per claim for CHAS Blue:

| Procedure | Maximum Subsidy (S$) |
|---|---|
| Consultation | 20.50 |
| Polishing | 20.50 |
| Scaling | 30.00 |
| Topical Fluoride | 20.50 |
| X-Ray | 11.00 |
| Extraction, Anterior | 28.50 |
| Extraction, Posterior | 68.50 |
| Filling, Simple | 30.00 |
| Filling, Complex | 50.00 |
| Re-cementation | 35.00 |
| Denture Reline / Repair | 75.00 |
| Permanent Crown | 615.00 |
| Removable Denture, Complete | 408.50 |
| Removable Denture, Partial, Simple | 304.00 |
| Removable Denture, Partial, Complex | 385.50 |
| Root Canal Treatment (Anterior) | 326.00 |
| Root Canal Treatment (Pre-molar) | 462.50 |
| Root Canal Treatment (Molar) | 584.50 |

Subsidies for the seven restorative procedures (permanent crown, removable dentures, and root canal treatments) were specifically increased in the October 2025 enhancement.

## What CHAS does *not* cover

CHAS dental subsidies cover **only** the 18 procedures listed above. Treatments outside that list are not covered, including:

- Dental implants
- Veneers
- Teeth whitening
- Orthodontic treatment (braces, clear aligners)
- Cosmetic procedures
- Periodontal surgery
- Most surgical procedures (these may be Medisave-claimable instead — see [subsidy-medisave-dental](subsidy-medisave-dental))

## Subsidy is per claim, not per visit

Each subsidy cap applies per procedure claim. A single visit involving multiple procedures (e.g. consultation + scaling + polishing + filling) generates separate subsidy claims for each, each subject to its own cap.

## How to use the subsidy

1. Verify your CHAS card status — current cards are valid for two years before renewal.
2. Find a participating CHAS dental clinic via [chas.sg](https://www.chas.sg).
3. Present your physical or digital CHAS card at the clinic before treatment.
4. The clinic processes the claim and the subsidy is deducted from your bill.

## Combining with the MOH fee benchmark

From 1 October 2025, CHAS dental clinics are required to display the MOH fee benchmarks alongside their own price lists. This means at a CHAS clinic you can see:

- The clinic's quoted fee for the procedure
- The MOH benchmark range for that procedure
- The CHAS subsidy you are eligible for
- The expected co-payment (clinic fee minus subsidy)

This transparency is the intention of the enhanced scheme.

## Important caveats

- The CHAS subsidy is a maximum cap per claim, not a guaranteed amount — the actual subsidy received depends on the clinic's bill and the scheme rules.
- The chatbot cannot confirm individual entitlement amounts. Verify via [chas.sg](https://www.chas.sg) or with the clinic's billing team. See [guard-refusal-subsidy-entitlement](guard-refusal-subsidy-entitlement).
- Pre-existing fee benchmarks displayed at a CHAS clinic may not exactly match a clinic's own historical pricing — discrepancies should be discussed with the clinic.

# CHAS Orange — dental subsidies

CHAS Orange is the higher-income-band tier of the Community Health Assist Scheme. Historically it covered only seven restorative procedures for dental; from **1 October 2025**, the scheme was significantly expanded to also cover the 10 basic and preventive procedures previously available only to CHAS Blue, Pioneer Generation, and Merdeka Generation cardholders.

This is a major change for routine dental care among middle-income Singaporeans — if you have a CHAS Orange card, scaling, polishing, fillings, extractions, X-rays, and consultation are now subsidised at participating CHAS clinics.

## Eligibility

CHAS Orange is generally available to Singapore Citizens with monthly household income per person of approximately S$2,001 to S$2,800 (or, for households with no income, an Annual Value of home approximately S$21,001 to S$31,000). Eligibility can be checked or applied for at [chas.sg](https://www.chas.sg) or via Healthhub.

## What changed in October 2025

Two changes took effect on 1 October 2025:

1. **Subsidy extension** — CHAS Orange holders gained subsidy access (up to S$45.50 per claim) for **10 basic and preventive procedures** previously not covered by CHAS Orange:
   - Consultation
   - Polishing
   - Scaling
   - Topical Fluoride
   - X-Ray
   - Extraction (Anterior and Posterior)
   - Filling (Simple and Complex)
   - Re-cementation

2. **Increased subsidy** — CHAS Orange holders received increased subsidies (alongside CHAS Blue, PG, and MG) for the **seven restorative procedures**:
   - Permanent Crown
   - Removable Denture, Complete
   - Removable Denture, Partial Simple and Complex
   - Root Canal Treatment (Anterior, Pre-molar, Molar)

# Flexi-MediSave for dental

**Flexi-MediSave** is a Medisave-funded enhancement, announced as part of the September 2025 dental package, that takes effect from **mid-2026**. It allows Singapore Citizens and Permanent Residents aged 60 and above to use Medisave funds for two specific non-surgical dental procedures — historically not Medisave-claimable.

This is a meaningful change for seniors managing the cost of crowns and root canals, which are among the most expensive routine dental procedures.

## Eligibility

- Singapore Citizen or Permanent Resident
- Aged **60 years or above**
- Sufficient Medisave balance (own account or eligible family member's account)

## Coverage

- **Up to S$400 per year** can be drawn from Medisave under Flexi-MediSave for dental
- Covers two procedure categories:
  - **Permanent Crowns**
  - **Root Canal Treatments**
- Available at:
  - **CHAS dental clinics**
  - **Public healthcare institutions** (restructured hospitals, NDCS, NUCOH)

## What this means in practice

For a senior who is also a Pioneer Generation or Merdeka Generation cardholder having a permanent crown at a CHAS clinic:

1. **MOH benchmark** for a private permanent crown: S$750–S$1,400 (excluding GST)
2. **PG / MG / CHAS Blue subsidy** at the CHAS clinic: S$615–S$625 deducted from the bill
3. **Flexi-MediSave**: up to S$400 of the remaining balance can be drawn from Medisave (within the annual S$400 cap)
4. **Remaining out-of-pocket**: typically a small balance after both subsidies

For a similar case at a public healthcare institution: the same Flexi-MediSave S$400 can be applied to the institution's subsidised rate.

## Annual cap

The S$400 limit is **per year**, not per procedure. If you receive both a crown (drawing S$400) and a root canal (drawing S$0 because the cap is reached) in the same year, the second procedure cannot draw additional Flexi-MediSave funds until the next year.

This makes timing relevant: for treatment plans involving multiple crowns or root canals, spreading procedures across calendar years allows more Flexi-MediSave use.

## What's *not* covered by Flexi-MediSave

- Procedures other than permanent crowns and root canal treatments
- Crowns or root canals at non-CHAS private clinics
- Patients younger than 60

For other dental needs, see:

- [subsidy-medisave-dental](subsidy-medisave-dental) — Medisave for surgical procedures (any age)
- [subsidy-chas-blue](subsidy-chas-blue), [subsidy-chas-orange](subsidy-chas-orange), [subsidy-pioneer-generation](subsidy-pioneer-generation), [subsidy-merdeka-generation](subsidy-merdeka-generation) — CHAS for the 18 covered procedures

## CHAS clinic accreditation requirement

As part of the Flexi-MediSave roll-out, all CHAS dental clinics will be required to onboard the Flexi-MediSave scheme by **31 December 2026**, and dental practitioners in those clinics must be Medisave-accredited. CHAS clinics not accredited by that date will be offboarded from the CHAS scheme.

In practice this means most CHAS clinics will accept Flexi-MediSave from mid-2026 onward; if a clinic does not yet accept it, ask before booking.

## Important caveats

- The chatbot cannot confirm individual Medisave balance or annual usage. Verify via [cpf.gov.sg](https://www.cpf.gov.sg) or the clinic.
- Eligibility for Flexi-MediSave is independent of CHAS card status — a senior who is not a CHAS card holder can still use Flexi-MediSave at a CHAS clinic.

# Merdeka Generation Package — dental subsidies
